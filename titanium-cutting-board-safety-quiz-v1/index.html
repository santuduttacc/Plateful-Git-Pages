Plastic Boards Shed Microplastics Into Food

Every cut creates microscopic plastic fragments that break off into your food.

These microplastics can accumulate in the body and have been linked to inflammation, hormone disruption, and long-term health concerns.

Wood and Bamboo Boards Trap Harmful Bacteria

Wood and bamboo are porous. Knife grooves absorb moisture, raw meat juices, and bacteria below the surface.

This bacteria can transfer into food and may contribute to foodborne illness, digestive irritation, and repeated exposure — especially concerning for children and older adults.

Glass Boards Chip and Transfer Glass Into Food

Cutting on glass causes microscopic glass chipping with repeated use.

These fragments can enter food and irritate the digestive tract or cause internal micro-injuries over time.

Glass also severely dulls knives.

Steel Isn’t Antibacterial — and It’s Harsh on Knives

Steel boards are not naturally antibacterial. Scratches allow bacteria to survive and spread between foods.

Steel is also similar in strength to knife blades, meaning they grind against each other — accelerating dulling and blade damage.
